Summary: The purpose of this study is to develop a grading system for esophageal varices using capsule endoscopy in patients with portal hypertension and cirrhosis.

Detailed description: Up to 35% of patients with cirrhosis will develop esophageal varices, which carry with them a 1-year and 3-year bleeding rate of 33% and 41%, respectively. Screening for varices with a video endoscopy is limited by its cost, patient compliance, and the risks of sedating a patient with portal hypertension. Capsule endoscopy is a novel method that may reduce the costs and increase compliance for screening. The investigators propose to screen 100 consecutive patients with advanced cirrhosis for esophageal varices. A capsule endoscopy and video endoscopy will be done on the same day. The varices seen on video endoscopy will be graded using a standard grading scale. Both capsule and video endoscopy images will be assessed in a blinded fashion by 4 independent investigators. Finally, the video endoscopic images will be compared to capsule endoscopy to develop a capsule endoscopy grading system that corresponds to video endoscopy. The primary endpoint of the study will be to develop a grading system for esophageal varices using capsule endoscopy. Secondary endpoints of the study include sensitivity, specificity, inter- and intraobserver variation of variceal grading using capsule endoscopy; patient acceptance as assessed on a visual analogue scale; and a cost comparison (facility fees + professional fees) of the two screening methods.

Eligibility criteria: Inclusion Criteria: 1. Aged between 18 and 70 years old, male and female patients are both eligible. 2. Meet the diagnostic criteria of chronic hepatitis B (CHB): HBsAg positive for more than 6 months, detectable HBV DNA, accompanied by abnormal liver function, hepatic inflammation or liver fibrosis, and other liver diseases are excluded. 3. Diagnosed with metabolic associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) confirmed by imaging examination (ultrasound, CT or MRI) with hepatic steatosis ≥5%; patients meet at least one metabolic abnormality standard: BMI ≥25kg/m², fasting glucose ≥5.6mmol/L or type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia; patients without other etiologies of liver injury such as alcoholic liver disease and drug-induced liver injury. 4. Classified into 5 TCM syndrome types by experienced physicians through four diagnostic methods: spleen damp-heat syndrome, liver depression and spleen deficiency syndrome, spleen deficiency and phlegm-damp syndrome, damp-heat accumulation syndrome, damp-heat blood stasis syndrome. 5. Patients fully understand the study content and voluntarily sign the written informed consent form. 6. Complete clinical laboratory, imaging and four-diagnosis record data available for retrospective analysis. Exclusion Criteria: 1. Patients with decompensated cirrhosis (Child-Pugh grade C) or liver failure. 2. Complicated with end-stage renal disease, malignant tumors, severe cardiovascular diseases and other severe systemic diseases. 3. Pregnant or lactating women. 4. Patients with mental disorders who cannot cooperate with data collection and clinical evaluation. 5. Long-term users of immunosuppressants. 6. Patients with incomplete medical records that cannot support complete index analysis. 7. Patients with other confounding liver diseases interfering with the judgment of MAFLD and CHB.
